Where the app actually stands today

What is inside Lifestead, and how it is put together

Lifestead is an everything-about-a-person's-life app, tagline "Everything in your life has a place." It started with, and runs deepest on, food and autoimmune health: 19 tracked conditions, each a target for the same depth, alone or combined. It is also for somebody who needs an outside place to hold the details of daily living so they stop taking up room in their head, through Life, Schedules, reminders and capture. Garden, budget, family, interests, learning and the body are all in scope.

Its job in food and health is finding the personal patterns between what someone eats and how their body responds, and doing that work for the person. Records stay on the person's devices, and no company server holds them.

How the app is navigated

There's no bottom tab bar. The whole app runs on a hub-and-spoke model built specifically for this project.

TabHub

A central popup menu (reached from a floating button) replaces a persistent bottom tab bar. It shows every top-level tab at once, color-coded by its own jewel tone sampled from the app's commissioned butterfly artwork, plus left/right swipe gestures for moving between tabs directly.

LensHub

Most tabs (Life, Food, Garden, Interests, Schedules, Signals, Insights, Trends, Reports) have their own internal "lenses" — sub-views reached through a second, page-local popup menu rather than a wall of sub-tabs. Picking a tab from TabHub briefly auto-opened that tab's LensHub, which stopped on 2026-08-30 by direct instruction; the box in the lower corner names the button to tap instead. The plumbing is left in place and switched off in one line inside hooks/useAutoOpenLensHubSignal.ts, because the same query param is still what tells a deep link which tab was deliberately chosen.

The ten tabs, in menu and swipe order: Home, Life, Food, Garden, Interests, Schedules, Signals, Insights, Trends, Reports, then Profile and Info in the same menu. One list (constants/tabs.ts) drives the menu, the swipe order and the header dots together. The Digest tab was taken apart on 2026-09-19: reading now lives on Life (Conditions, Health Literacy, Earth Matters, Search Reading), Garden (Horticulture) and Food (recipes). Which hand works the navigation is a live setting on each device, and the hubs mirror to match it. A lens is a subject holding several bands behind the fold, and a thing is defined where it lives and gets its timeline on Schedules.

Home gained its own corner menu in the same pass, replacing a shortcut that had made the corner button mean "pick what you want to see here" on nine tabs and "go to the Digest" on one, back when there was a Digest tab. It is deliberately not a scroll-to: every section on Home is a window onto a function living in another tab, so picking one goes to that function, exactly as tapping the card does. The single exception is the Library (the flip cards, in a purple group of their own since 1.0.41.14, named Library under a library icon since 1.0.69.3), whose cards are the content rather than a shortcut, so it scrolls to itself. Each icon wears the colour of the tab it leads to, so the grouping and the colouring compound and the grid reads as blocks.

The menus also stopped opening themselves. Picking a tab used to auto-open that tab's own lens picker; an on-page prompt naming the corner button replaced it, which is where that prompt had sat before the auto-open was introduced. On 2026-09-24 the on-page copy came out again as a duplicate, leaving the box in the lower corner as the one place that sentence lives. And the active ring and menu border now follow the chosen Ground Theme rather than a fixed teal, lightened by a measured amount first: taken raw, the theme's own button colour measures between 2.26:1 and 3.20:1 against the menu surface, under the 3:1 floor on four of the five themes.

Since 2026-09-12 every screen takes one look, worked out on Home first and then passed through Food and Schedules: full-width bands with a 4px accent bar in the tab's colour and hairlines above and below, no right edge, each one folding to a single row carrying its name and count until tapped, with what is left open remembered across launches. Rows inside a band are inset boxes at one standard gap, forms stay open as plain band boxes, and a menu row is an action band. Each tab's resting screen carried one such box naming the tab with its icon until 2026-09-24, and the box in the lower corner shows the same icon beside "Tap the {tab} button in the corner to pick a tool", the tab name in that tab's colour, at a fixed size on every tab.

On 2026-09-24 (1.0.51.1) every tab's resting screen was emptied on purpose. The signpost box at the top went, because the corner box was already saying the same thing and one sentence does not need two places. Food's resting "Desktop", the one tab piloting that pattern, went with it, since every row it drew was already a row of the My Foods popup. What is left is the background, and a tab's background choice now reaches it: a tab set to Off, Generic or a photo somebody added shows that from the moment the tab opens, while the default leaves the resting screen to the single shared scene rather than bringing that tab's bundled image forward. The reason for emptying them is that these screens are meant to become where a person's use of the app shows over time, and nothing can grow on a screen already full of links to somewhere else. That growth is designed and not built; the design is recorded in the project's CLAUDE.md as open next step 29.

Why this shape: the app's own build history shows two earlier, more conventional attempts (a persistent tab bar, then an auto-opening popup on every screen arrival) both getting reverted before this version — this hub-and-spoke pattern is a deliberate, tested design, not a placeholder.

Turning every section off shows a plain explanatory message instead of a blank screen.

The welcome card itself collapses into a small floating badge in the top-left corner 30 seconds after the app opens (or immediately on swiping to another tab), staying out of the way of the content beneath it, and expands back to full size on tap before auto-collapsing again.

As of 2026-08-21, the header also carries a first, deliberately small piece of a longer-term "growth vine" plan: one dot per non-Home tab under the header title, showing at a glance which tabs have real activity, plus a reserved band underneath for a future growing mark per tab as real milestones (every Food builder used, a first logged meal, and so on) are reached. Tapping the header's own "{name}'s Lifestead" title opens a Timeline screen — still a real, honest stub today, not yet the full horizontal life-history view the plan describes.

Your Guide

The guided way through the app. Called Your Guide in the code, shown as Your Guide since 1.0.65.5, with its sections named for what they hold.

How it works

Built

The heading reads Start Here at first, then Getting Set Up, then All Set, More When You Want It. It starts with six sections: What Matters to You, Keeping Your Records Safe, What You Take, What Your Days Hold, What You Eat, Drink and Feel, and What Your Records Show. What Matters to You asks "What parts of your life do you want Lifestead to follow?" and each part chosen (Health, Food, Movement, Home, Garden, Money, Routines, Work, Family, Interests) can add a section after those six. Choosing never hides anything anywhere in the app.

An item is done when its record exists. It carries the date that record went in, and if the record goes it reopens and says so. There is no "step 3 of 8", no percentage, no praise and no blame, and every item links to the exact tab and lens where it is done.

It lives in five places: a card on Home that can be folded and moved but not turned off, since it is how somebody finds their way back to a step that dropped off; a full page; an entry in Profile; a "Where this fits in Your Guide" line at the foot of every help sheet; and the empty states of Pattern Finder, Trends and Reports, which say which step would give them something to show.

Guides for every part of life

Since 1.0.51.7 each part of life has a guide, step by step, and since 1.0.51.8 the steps are grouped by when they happen (Start here, Every day, Later on, When it happens) and ordered toward the first thing the part can show back. Each step can be read Short or Step by step, a choice that is remembered.

Back to Your Guide

Since 1.0.51.9, any screen opened from a Your Guide line carries one pill in the top right that goes back to the same place in the guide, so following a step never means finding the way back by hand.

Walk me through it

Since 1.0.51.10, a step can be walked. A strip at the top of the screen shows one line at a time (tap the round button, pick Life, open My Meds) and moves on by itself as each screen is reached. It has Next, Back, Your Guide and Stop, folds to a single line when it is in the way, and watches for the record: once it exists, the strip says it is saved and what it leads to.

The button it names is outlined

Since 1.0.51.11, on the Start here and Every day steps, the button a walk line names is drawn with an outline on the screen itself: the round button, the corner button and My Items get a round one, anything else a box. An outline takes up no room, so marking a button never moves the screen around it, and nothing is outlined once the record is saved or the strip is closed.

By tab

Since 1.0.52.3 the guide also reads by tab: where records go in (Life, Schedules, Signals, Food, Garden) and what records give back (Insights, Trends, Reports), each tab saying what it gives and naming anything from another tab that has to exist first.

An interview, then a tour

Since 1.0.52.5 it opens as an interview, one question at a time: conditions, neurodivergence, parts of life, where to start, meds and supplements, the stage of each staged condition, and eating style. A tour follows, and since 1.0.52.6 it describes each whole tab: what it does for you, what you can count on it for, every lens in one line, and the fastest way in, the first step not yet done marked Start with this.

The output lenses

In 1.0.52.7 every lens marked Build on the inputs-to-outputs map was built: nine on Trends, six on Insights and seven new reports, all listed in the tour. Each reads records that were already being kept.

Conditions moved here from the Digest tab on 2026-09-19 ("we are moving the conditions all to Life"). It renders every condition's reading in the System Recipes layout: one fold band per condition with its full-colour icon, topic folds and subgroups inside, entries opening in place, one search box over the page. Three lists never mix: My Conditions (Profile's selections), Family (a local roster, whose members' conditions join the Meal Plan's scope when their include-in-meal-plan switch is on), and Other Conditions (the "curious about" list, readable here and never feeding scoring, planning or advisories).

Health Literacy, Earth Matters and Search Reading joined the same day when the Digest tab was taken apart. Health Literacy is the condition-agnostic layer that used to be called Basic Health, with the Glossary as a band inside it; Earth Matters is the food-system and planet reading; Search Reading searches every entry in the library, with hits expanding in place and a hit that lives on Garden or Food going there and arriving open. All three render on the same fold bands as Conditions.

It shipped as an honest empty room first. "A ton of things" was named without naming which things, so a guessed lens set would have meant building something to tear out; the tab was complete as a tab, and its single Overview lens said plainly what it was for and that nothing was in it yet.

Finances is the largest area, and it rests on one decision: it reads what already exists rather than asking anyone to retype it. Priced grocery trips and hands-on therapy costs are read where they live and copied nowhere, because copying is exactly how a corrected price ends up disagreeing with a stale duplicate. The first version was built without looking at a single real finance app and it showed; the rebuild covers accounts and net worth, per-category budget limits, debt payoff simulated month by month with a rolling commitment, sinking funds, goals, and income streams. Two things make it worth having inside a health app rather than replaceable by Monarch: a health-money layer (deductible and out-of-pocket standing, HSA versus FSA, an Explanation of Benefits check whose whole value is what it leaves out, and cost per condition) and a refusal to invent figures.

That refusal is the throughline. A goal costs money, or time, or goods, and those are never blended into one percentage, because $500 and 20 hours with half of each done is two facts rather than "50% done". Hours are never converted to dollars at an assumed rate. A market-rate account is measured rather than projected, and money paid in is asked about separately, since a balance rising from 40,000 to 46,000 could be a 30% return or a 5,000 deposit. A trade of surplus produce is valued only when a recorded price exists from that person's own past shopping, and then only as an avoided cost, never as income.

Work covers both readings of one question: benefits going unclaimed, and the value in the work itself. It names no country, statute or scheme anywhere, and a test enforces that, because almost everything here is jurisdiction-specific enough that a confident claim would be wrong for most readers. Autonomy, competence and relatedness are attributed to Self-Determination Theory rather than presented as the app's idea; a first draft had four and folded meaning in as though it belonged to the same framework, which it does not.

Upkeep deliberately does not reuse the bill scheduling beside it, and the distinction is exact: a bill is calendar-anchored, so rent arrives on the 1st whether or not you did anything, while a service is last-done-anchored, so a boiler serviced in March is next due the following March rather than next January. Reusing the bill machinery would have announced a service due in January regardless of the one three months earlier.

Emergency & Essentials leads with a warning rather than a feature, and it is not dismissible: an app on a locked phone is not reachable by a paramedic, and anyone who comes away believing this replaces a bracelet or the medical ID their phone already has is worse off. Checking the app rather than assuming turned up the gap that mattered most: it tracked food allergies and had no concept of a drug allergy at all, which on an emergency card is arguably the highest-stakes line there is. An absent field is absent rather than printed blank, since "Drug allergies: none recorded" reads as "no allergies" to someone scanning it.

Kitchen is what is actually in the house, fed by three sources: garden and fermentation harvests, which carry a measured remaining amount, and everything added by hand or put there by ticking a grocery line off. The line it draws is the same honesty rule the grocery list already turns on. A harvest can be subtracted because it is drawn down as it gets used; a past purchase cannot, because nothing decrements it as it gets eaten, so it surfaces as "you bought this 2 days ago, worth checking" and never as an amount. Household consumables live in their own inventory beside the food one, since almost everything the app does with a food is meaningless for bleach. Making a dish moves stock both ways, and it is never silent: the plan is shown, then confirmed, because saving a recipe is not cooking it.

My Meds arrived from Schedules on 2026-09-13 and is the one place a prescription, an over-the-counter drug or a supplement is added, edited, paused or removed: pick a prescription or OTC item from a cited reference list where one exists or enter it yourself, give a supplement its nutrients and, where the app has researched them, the specific form (magnesium glycinate against oxide, since the form changes how well it absorbs), and see when to take it, what to avoid pairing it with, and how much of that nutrient food already covered today. Editing came here with it from the two Schedules lenses that used to carry their own copies of the same forms. It sets no times itself: each med has a Schedule it button that opens Schedules > Meds with that med ready, which is the whole reason it sits on Life beside Kitchen rather than on Schedules. What you take is a thing you own; when you take it is a schedule.

Days Until arrived on 2026-09-22 (1.0.49.7) from a direct request: a counter "should be something that is also available in a free form allowing the user to create their own Days Until for something that we don't have covered in the app." A counter can now be about a passport in the post, a course starting, a cast coming off or a warranty running out, and this lens is every counter of either kind on one list, ordered by when it lands, a garden one naming its area and a free one showing whatever was written under it. Starting one here picks a garden area or Anything. It is a second table rather than the garden one with the area left blank, deliberately: a garden counter travels to Past Areas with its area and counts toward whether that area still holds records, and neither is true of a free one. One piece of arithmetic serves both, so both count whole calendar days, both read Today on the day, both keep counting as "N days over" rather than disappearing, and both raise the same reminder that morning, differing only in which tab a tap on it lands on. Garden > Days Until is unchanged and stays about the garden.

Food

Twelve focused builders for constructing a real meal from raw ingredients — deliberately not a generic "log a branded product" tracker. The app's own stated direction favors home cooking over commercial/pre-made items; a real, dedicated "Scan a Product" barcode flow (see the Roadmap section below) handles a genuine store-bought or packaged item separately, rather than folding it into these 12 whole-food builders.

The twelve builders

Built
Meal Sides Salads & Bowls Smoothies Fermentation Beverages Snacks Baked Goods Soups Sauces Handhelds Desserts

Meal Builder is the assembler — it doesn't add raw ingredients itself, it pulls together saved output from the other eleven (pick how much of a saved Side or Salad was actually eaten, log it as one real meal). It can log something right now, favorite it, or schedule it for later the same day.

Every one of the other eleven follows the same real shape: name it, add ingredients from a category-scoped food search, get a live nutrient/6-Dimensions preview, then save it as a standalone record and/or a reusable favorite. Several carry their own real, specific checks — a raw-goitrogenic-load warning when two or more raw cruciferous ingredients are combined (Salads & Bowls, Smoothies), and non-blocking advisory rows for alcohol, coffee, and juice content where relevant.

Beverages, Fermentation, Soups, Sauces, and Desserts (the five builders whose own category allowlist includes Alcohol) also carry a real, inline ABV/residual-sugar/pours calorie calculator — a genuine chemistry estimate (grams of ethanol from volume × pours × ABV% × 0.789, calories at 7 kcal/g, plus a real, cited alcohol-retention-by-cooking-method table for anything simmered or reduced) for the case a real bottle — an off-catalog proof, a homemade wine, more than one pour, a reduced sauce — doesn't match any single database row closely enough. As of 2026-08-11, filling in Volume and ABV makes the calculator's own real result the actual tracked value for that ingredient, auto-filling Quantity/Unit and overriding the plain database-row lookup for calories/carbs specifically (every other nutrient stays at zero, matching how thoroughly distillation strips out non-ethanol content) — a real, standing note above Quantity/Unit says so plainly the moment it's active. Deliberately excludes beer (real brewing chemistry means dextrins/starches survive fermentation as unfermented carbs a formula like this would systematically undercount).

As of 2026-08-13, tapping Beverages fresh (not editing or reusing a saved one) opens a real subcategory picker first — Cocktails & Mixology, Infusions & Brews, Juices & Nectars, Hydration & Wellness, Performance & Protein all scope the ingredient search narrower to match, while Smoothies and Fermentation redirect straight into those two already-existing standalone builders instead.

As of 2026-08-14, ten of the eleven direct-ingredient sub-builders also open with a real "Or Start From a Recipe" option — now 300 real, curated recipes (started at 12, Salad/Smoothie only) already assembled from real ingredients, each independently verified against the live database, tappable directly or reachable from a matching "Build This Recipe" button on the Recipes lens on Food (the reading category moved there on 2026-09-18). Fermentation Builder's own recipes carry real, specific bacterial strains too (a new two-table addition to the reference database, reusing already-published, already-cited strain research from the reading library) via a real multi-select strain picker.

As of 2026-08-24, 194 of those 300 recipes are the real 6-Week Whole-Food Meal Plan — a full 42-day breakfast/lunch/dinner rotation with zero repeated meals, plus 106 more built as a full, parallel Vegan and Vegetarian version of the same plan (every real vegetable, aromatic, and seasoning from the omnivore dish carried over unchanged, only the animal protein swapped for tofu, tempeh, seitan, a legume, or a "meaty" mushroom). The fixed 42-day rotation itself was retired on 2026-09-13 when the two meal generators became one (see Schedules below); every one of those recipes stays in the library and is what the remaining generator draws from.

As of 2026-08-25, a direct report ("Bison could be something a person wouldn't really be able to get") turned into a full audit of all 210 distinct ingredients actually used across the 300 curated recipes for real supermarket availability. Bison swapped to Beef Top Sirloin (3 recipes, an invisible 1:1 substitution — the two score identically across every practically relevant six-dimension sub-criterion). The data underneath it all

Every tab above draws from the same bundled reference database — this is what makes the food side of the app work at all.

26,749 foods, 9 national sources plus a Derived set

USDA, Canada's CNF, the UK's CoFID, Germany's BLS, Australia's AFCD, France's Ciqual, and Japan's MEXT — bundled as a compact SQLite asset, not a live network call. Every food carries up to 35 nutrient values and a real per-source-citation trail for its own D1–D6 scoring. Norway (Matvaretabellen) and Sweden (Livsmedelsverket) joined as a real 8th and 9th source in 2026-08-10 — 2,121 and 2,606 foods respectively, both genuinely imported and correct, but deliberately kept hidden from the app's own default browsing until a real name-translation pass exists, since every one of those entries is currently untranslated Norwegian/Swedish.

The 6 Dimensions of Food Friendliness (6-DFF)

A real, Hashimoto's-specific scoring rubric across six dimensions (goitrogenic load, gluten, additives, processing, fat quality, and more), each sub-criterion individually cited and evidence-tiered — not a black-box score.

Reuse-first condition scoring

Rather than build a whole new scoring system per condition, most of the 19 tracked conditions reuse an existing 6-DFF sub-criterion under a condition-specific framing (the same Omega-3 data reads very differently for Rheumatoid Arthritis vs. Psoriasis vs. Prostate Health). A handful of conditions (Type 1 Diabetes, Chronic Kidney Disease, Cardiovascular Disease) needed one genuinely new, real, data-backed sub-criterion of their own.

A living Reference Database Audit tool

A separate, standalone tool used to hand-review and curate which of the 26,749 foods are actually shown, hide near-duplicates, fix miscategorizations, and bulk-apply thousands of curation decisions directly against the live database. A real, ongoing, iterative workflow — each exported decision batch (over 1,300 real decisions in the most recent one, 2026-08-10) gets resolved against whatever the live database actually looks like at apply time, not assumed to still match the state the tool's own view was built from, so review and apply can keep happening in separate sessions without the two ever silently drifting out of sync.

Architecture: built vs. planned

Where it stands today

Built
  • Delivery: JavaScript reaches phones over the air and native changes need a rebuild. Developer and Test versions stay matched until it is time to give the app to testers, and the Windows installers are built from the same code.
  • The tester version: opens only on an approved device, after an agreement and a signed approval. An ended approval keeps every record and only covers them.
  • The database only grows: the code that runs at startup adds tables, columns and indexes and never drops, renames or empties one, so testers keep their records across every update.
  • App Lock and the vault: on phone and desktop. The vault holds records only, never tools. Medicines, conditions and appointments in it are only hidden by screens, so safety warnings and scoring keep reading them.
  • The locked phone: every notification is answered or dismissed by tapping on the locked phone, and capture over the lock screen never opens the database. What was captured is sealed to the lock's public key and written at the next unlock.
  • Desktop and sync: the same code in an Electron window. A person's two devices stay in step through a folder on the person's cloud drive, one encrypted copy per device. Every arrival is merged record by record, one device has the session at a time, and a save sends only what changed.
  • Between two people: the same merge, on an allowlist. Nothing crosses unless a relationship names it, and today that is only the shopping list.
  • Free and paid: the free/paid board is the authority. Safety is never behind a paywall on any tier: the emergency card, med and supplement timing warnings, and allergy cautions.
  • Languages of the app: one app carries every language, and the English stays in the source. Mexican Spanish is finished and follows the phone's language by default. Every entry is Claude's translation, not yet checked by a native speaker.

Named and not built: bees and beekeeping, the Diary, working life and a resume, family health history with a family tree, Contacts, what grows on the freed tab screens, the external data program, and a life's Inside Story. The unified whole-foods database is a separate project not wired into the app. The accounts below are history and stay as they were written.

How an update actually reaches a phone. As of 2026-08-28/29 the app also ships as a standalone build installed directly on real test phones, with no live development server involved, and JS-only changes reach those phones over the air without a reinstall.
